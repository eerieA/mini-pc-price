## Reading (added 2026-09-25)

Three of the five comments carry no evidentiary weight: "too good to be true,"
"the website looks cheaply made," and a guess that the vendor is also on eBay.
None involves a transaction. §3's framework exists to discard exactly this.

**One comment is a real report.** `Ok-Essay-3489`, 2025-12-13: ordered a laptop,
no tracking link, status stuck at "label created by shipper" for about a month,
calls and emails ignored, cancellation request ignored. The laptop arrived
expedited only after a PayPal dispute was escalated. Their inference -- that the
vendor may be counting on buyers giving up -- is speculation, but the sequence of
events is first-hand.

**One comment cuts the other way**: the domain has been registered since 2017,
which argues against outright fraud.

### Corroboration beyond this thread

The thread's poster named two review aggregators, and they disagree:

| Source | Score | n | Who can post |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trustpilot (ca.trustpilot.com) | 1.9 / 5 | 55 | anyone |
| Judge.me | not read | -- | merchant-installed Shopify app |

Trustpilot, checked 2026-09-25: **77% one-star**, 11% five-star. The recurring
complaints are shipping far beyond the stated 7-14 business days, support
ignoring email and phone, refunds refused, and some wrong or damaged items. That
matches the Reddit report's sequence rather than merely rhyming with it.

The disagreement between the two aggregators is structural, not mysterious.
Judge.me is a review app the merchant installs and configures on their own
Shopify store; Trustpilot is open to the public. A store-controlled widget and an
open one are not two independent readings of the same thing, so the more
favourable number is also the weaker evidence. (The Judge.me page did not render
for capture, so its figures are not recorded here -- the point stands on what the
widget *is*, not on its score.)

### Why this is not the same kind of evidence as the other transcripts

`research/` otherwise holds individual reports, and §3 is right to hold those
loosely. A 55-review aggregate at a stable URL is different in kind: it is
re-checkable by anyone, it is not a self-selected handful, and it can be
re-verified before a purchase. That puts it closer to the Uniway markup finding
(durable, at a URL) than to the REFURB.io gate (three comments).

The failure mode is the same one that gated REFURB.io: fulfillment delay plus
unresponsive support, recoverable only through payment-processor pressure.
